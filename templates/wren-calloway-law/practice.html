Divorce, step by step

Since 2022, divorce in England and Wales is “no-fault”: nobody has to be blamed. Here’s how it works.

  1. Application

    One of you, or both together, applies online. We prepare and check everything, then send it to the court.

    Week 1

  2. Twenty-week reflection period

    A pause the law requires. It’s the right time to agree how to split money and property, which we help with.

    Weeks 2–21

  3. Conditional order

    The court confirms you’re entitled to divorce. Financial agreements can now be approved.

    Around week 22

  4. Final order

    Six weeks and a day later at the earliest, you can apply for the final order, which ends the marriage.

    From week 28

Arrangements for children

Most parents agree where their children will live and how they’ll spend time with each of them, sometimes with the help of a mediator. We can put that agreement in writing. If you can’t agree, we’ll explain how a court would look at it, and represent you if it gets that far.

Questions

How long does a divorce take?

Usually seven to nine months. The law requires at least 20 weeks between the application and the conditional order, then six weeks and a day before the final order. Sorting out finances can take longer, and we’ll often wait for that before applying for the final order.

Do we have to go to court?

Very rarely. Most couples agree, sometimes with help from mediation, and the court approves the agreement on paper. We’ll always try that first.

Is the court fee included in your price?

No. The court fee is paid to the court and is the same wherever you go. We’ll tell you the current amount and whether you qualify for help with it.

Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid for family cases is limited, mostly to cases involving domestic abuse or mediation. We’ll check on your first call and point you to the right place if you qualify.
